The Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) is a monthly, tax-free payment for people aged 65 or older who receive the Old Age Security (OAS) pension and have a low income. How much it pays depends on your marital status and on last year’s income, and Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) publishes the exact amount for every income in five tables each quarter. Canada.ca now shows only the maximum amounts and sends you to its estimator; the full tables are published as open data, and CanQuery’s [OAS and GIS amounts lookup](/oas) finds your line in them.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) |
| What the tables give | The monthly GIS, Allowance or Allowance for the Survivor for each range of yearly income |
| Tables | Five, by marital status: one for a single person, three for couples, one for a survivor |
| Updated | Every quarter: January, April, July and October |
| Income used | Net income of the calendar year before each July’s recalculation (2025 for July 2026 to June 2027) |
| Licence | Open Government Licence – Canada |

## Find your amount

1. Open the [OAS and GIS amounts lookup](/oas) and choose your situation. Each choice is one of the five official tables.
2. Type your yearly income. For a single person it is your own income; for a couple it is the two incomes together. Leave out the OAS pension and the GIS themselves.
3. Read the monthly amount. For the GIS tables the page also shows the total with the full OAS pension, for ages 65 to 74 and for 75 and over.

The lookup works in your browser: the income you type is not sent to CanQuery.

For example, in the tables for October to December 2026, a single person with a yearly income of $18,000 is in the band from $18,000.00 to $18,023.99: the GIS is $212.49 a month, or $974.99 a month together with the full OAS pension at ages 65 to 74.

## Which table is yours

- **Table 1** is for a single, widowed or divorced person who receives the OAS pension. [Open Table 1](/oas/table-1).
- **Table 2** is for a person whose spouse or common-law partner receives the full OAS pension. [Open Table 2](/oas/table-2).
- **Table 3** is for a person whose spouse or common-law partner receives neither the OAS pension nor the Allowance. [Open Table 3](/oas/table-3).
- **Table 4** is for a couple in which one partner receives the OAS pension and the other, aged 60 to 64, receives the Allowance. It gives both the GIS and the Allowance. [Open Table 4](/oas/table-4).
- **Table 5** is for a surviving spouse or common-law partner aged 60 to 64, who receives the Allowance for the Survivor. [Open Table 5](/oas/table-5).

For a couple, the tables use the combined income of the two partners, and each GIS amount is for one person.

## Which income counts

Each July, ESDC recalculates GIS and Allowance payments from your net income of the previous calendar year, and that income is used until the next June: the tables for July 2026 to June 2027 all use income for 2025. So a payment can change in July because your income changed, not only because the amounts did. The OAS pension and the GIS are not counted in that income; in Table 4 the Allowance is not counted either, and in Table 5 the Allowance for the Survivor is not.

Service Canada determines the income used for your own benefit. If you are not sure what counts, use its [Old Age Security Benefits Estimator](https://estimateursv-oasestimator.digital.service.canada.ca/en) or contact Service Canada.

## When the amounts change

ESDC reviews OAS amounts in January, April, July and October so that they follow the cost of living, as measured by the Consumer Price Index. Amounts go up when the cost of living rises and do not go down when it falls. ESDC replaces its files every quarter, and CanQuery loads them as soon as they are published: the lookup always names the quarter it shows, and says so when that quarter has not started yet.

## What the tables do not tell you

- They assume the **full OAS pension**. Canada.ca says that if you receive a partial pension, because you lived in Canada for at least 10 but less than 40 years after age 18, you should not use these tables to determine your payment: contact Service Canada.
- They do not decide whether you qualify. Read the conditions for the [Guaranteed Income Supplement](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/guaranteed-income-supplement.html) and the [Allowance](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/guaranteed-income-supplement/allowance.html).
- They do not give payment dates. Canada.ca shows the next payment date on its Guaranteed Income Supplement page, and the maximum amounts for the quarter on its [Old Age Security payment amounts](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/old-age-security/payments.html) page.

## Work with the files

The same five tables are CSV files you can open as tables on CanQuery, filter and download: start with [Table 1 as a table](/resources/0a7918f0-6e8a-433b-9ea2-60e2b8674cb5), or see every file, including the Excel versions and the publisher’s data dictionary, on the [dataset page](/datasets/dfa4daf1-669e-4514-82cd-982f27707ed0).
