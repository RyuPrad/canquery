You want to know how many cases of measles or another notifiable disease were reported in Canada in a given year, or how a disease's rate changed over decades. The Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) collects these counts in the **Canadian Notifiable Disease Surveillance System (CNDSS)**, which covers about 60 nationally notifiable diseases back to 1924. CanQuery offers the open-data tables as live tables and links to PHAC's own tool for the latest years.

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Public Health Agency of Canada |
| What it counts | Reported cases of nationally notifiable diseases, as counts and rates |
| Tables on CanQuery | Annual counts and rates, 1924 to 2016; and by age group and sex, 1991 to 2016 |
| Latest years | Notifiable Diseases Online, with data up to 2024 |
| Updated | Annually, by PHAC |
| On CanQuery | Live tables in English and French, with search, filters, charts and CSV export of up to 10,000 rows |

## Explore the long series

1. [Open the CNDSS annual counts and rates table in CanQuery](/resources/211adf12-1213-4953-afda-846c33a10972). It covers 1924 to 2016.
2. Search for a disease by name, then sort by year to follow it over time.
3. Switch to the chart view to plot the count or the rate across years.
4. For a breakdown by age group and sex from 1991, use the [table with age-group and sex breakdown](/resources/51e30776-a6ca-4d0c-b643-d62b3dccc6a9).
5. Export the filtered rows as CSV, up to 10,000 rows per export.

CanQuery first shows a preview of PHAC's file and prepares the full table after your first interaction with the page, or when you choose to load it; later visits read the prepared copy.

## Get the latest years

The open-data tables end in 2016. For later years, use PHAC's [Notifiable Diseases Online](https://diseases.canada.ca/notifiable/), an interactive site built on the same surveillance system, which has data up to 2024. Its [data extracts](https://diseases.canada.ca/notifiable/extract-dataset) cover 1924 to 2024, and 1991 to 2024 with age group and sex. Diseases added to the national list in 2024, such as mpox and Powassan virus disease, are available for 2024 only.

## Read the numbers carefully

Rates are expressed per 100,000, using population estimates as the denominator; PHAC uses live births instead for congenital and perinatal diseases. PHAC lists several cautions for these data:

- reported cases may not represent all cases in the population, partly because provincial and territorial case definitions can differ from the national one;
- not every province or territory can report every disease in every year;
- only confirmed cases are included unless otherwise specified;
- counts and rates for a disease and year may change over time;
- changes to national case definitions and disease classifications affect long-term trends;
- data for 2020 and 2021 should be interpreted with caution because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Compare years only after checking whether the disease's definition or reporting changed in between, and quote the source year of the table you used.

## Check the official source

PHAC's [Notifiable Diseases Online](https://diseases.canada.ca/notifiable/) explains the system, lists the diseases and gives the current data. The Government of Canada's dataset pages describe the [1924 to 2016](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/f2a711f9-be80-4b98-a205-8ac9e5e0d126) and [1991 to 2016](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/b712dd07-f30d-463f-b743-26ab3465d91b) tables.

When you share a finding, include the [CanQuery dataset page](/datasets/f2a711f9-be80-4b98-a205-8ac9e5e0d126), the disease, the years and the date you consulted it.
